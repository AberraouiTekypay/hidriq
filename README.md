## 1. Executive Overview

HIDRIQ is a vendor-neutral water intelligence and optimization system designed to sit between physical environmental conditions and existing irrigation infrastructure.

Much of the world’s installed irrigation—spanning luxury hospitality resorts, golf courses, private estates, and agriculture—operates on rigid timer clocks (e.g. *Monday & Thursday at 06:00 for 25 minutes*). Yet botanical water demand fluctuates continuously with solar radiation, vapor pressure deficit, wind, temperature, effective rainfall, and root-zone physics.

HIDRIQ solves this with an unyielding physical question:
> **“Given what we know about this land, vegetation/crop, soil, weather, terrain, and irrigation infrastructure, how much water should be delivered, when, and where?”**

```
PROPERTY DIGITAL TWIN
        ↓
AGRONOMIC KNOWLEDGE ENGINE (FAO-56 Penman-Monteith)
        ↓
WATER DEMAND & SOIL WATER BALANCE ENGINE
        ↓

        ↓
USER / CONTROLLER INTEGRATION
        ↓
OBSERVATION & SENSORS
        ↓
LONGITUDINAL LEARNING ENGINE (Constrained Calibration)
```

## 2. Master V5 Key Architectural Pillars

### 2.1 Pure Deterministic Agronomic Engine (Zero LLM Hallucination)
- **Critical Product Rule:** Recommendations **MUST NOT be generated by an LLM or generic AI model**.
- Fully deterministic mathematical calculations based on **ASCE FAO-56 Penman-Monteith Reference Evapotranspiration ($ET_0$)**, dual crop coefficients ($K_c$), and USDA-SCS effective rainfall ($P_{eff}$) accounting for canopy interception and infiltration limits.
- Root-zone soil moisture accounting ($TAW = 1000 \cdot (\theta_{FC} - \theta_{WP}) \cdot Z_r$) and allowable depletion ($RAW = p \cdot TAW$).

### 2.2 Peer-Reviewed Vegetation & Crop Knowledge Library
- Scientifically grounded in **FAO-56, University of California ANR WUCOLS IV, and INRAA Mediterranean Irrigation Guidelines**.
- Distinguishes rooting depths, seasonal $K_c$, and depletion thresholds:
  - **Warm-Season Turf (*Cynodon dactylon*):** 15–35 cm root depth, $K_c=0.70$, $p=0.50$.
  - **Cool-Season Turf (*Festuca arundinacea*):** 25–45 cm root depth, $K_c=0.85$, $p=0.45$.
  - **Mature Centenary Olives (*Olea europaea*):** 80–180 cm deep root column, $K_c=0.35$, $p=0.65$ (Extreme drought tolerance).
  - **Citrus (*Citrus sinensis / limon*):** 45–90 cm root cluster, $K_c=0.70$, $p=0.50$.
  - **Oleander Screening Hedges (*Nerium oleander*):** 40–90 cm root depth, $K_c=0.50$, $p=0.60$.
  - **Mediterranean Aromatics (Lavender & Rosemary):** 30–60 cm root depth, $K_c=0.30$, $p=0.65$ (Xeric adaptation).
  - **Wine Grapes (*Vitis vinifera*):** 90–220 cm root depth, $K_c=0.55$, $p=0.60$.

### 2.3 Zone-Level Distinction: Never Treat Landscape as Homogeneous
- The engine **NEVER applies a turf irrigation model to trees**.
- In the Property Dashboard, Zone 01 (Bermuda Lawn) receives targeted pre-dawn replenishment, while Zone 02 (Mature Olive Trees) reports:
  > **"NO IRRIGATION REQUIRED TODAY.** Deep 120cm root column holds 140+ mm available moisture. Deferring watering prevents collar rot."

### 2.4 Irrigation Hardware Honesty & No Fabricated Precision
- If emitter precipitation rate is unknown, HIDRIQ **explicitly refuses to guess exact runtime minutes**:
  > *"Water requirement estimated (X mm / Y L), but irrigation runtime cannot yet be calculated reliably without precipitation rate."*
- Homeowners and commercial operators can calibrate nozzle specifications ($mm/h$) in 1-click to promote the metric from `UNKNOWN` to `MEASURED`.

### 2.5 Mandatory 8-Point Explainability Report
Every recommendation answers:
1. **Why is water required?** (Root-zone depletion percentage vs. $RAW$ stress threshold).
2. **How much water is required?** (Depth in mm and volume in Liters across zone $m^2$).
3. **Why now?** (Pre-dawn 05:30–07:00 AM window to eliminate evaporative drift).
4. **Why this zone specifically?** (Contrasting root depth and $K_c$ against adjacent zones).
5. **What data supports this recommendation?** (Telemetry provenance table).
6. **What assumptions were made?** (Root depth, soil texture, distribution uniformity, microclimate).
7. **How confident is HIDRIQ?** (Composite score breaking down vegetation, soil, irrigation, and weather quality).
8. **What would change this recommendation?** (Rain interlock $>5\text{ mm}$, temperature drops, in-situ soil tension probes).

### 2.6 Strict Data Provenance Throughout the Platform
Every metric displays an explicit badge:
- `MEASURED` · `USER_CONFIRMED` · `INFERRED` · `ESTIMATED` · `UNKNOWN`

### 2.7 Persistent Digital Property Asset (`USER -> PROPERTIES[]`)
- Users register a property **once**. HIDRIQ remembers it. Returning users land immediately in their asset command center.
- Multi-property switcher: Marbella Villa, Madrid House, Mallorca Villa.
- Interactive 4-Overlay Digital Twin Canvas: Water Status, Vegetation Health, Irrigation State, Thermal/ET₀ Heatmap.
- Longitudinal Timeline and Visual Change Detection with non-alarmist investigation prompts.

## 3. The 20 Non-Negotiable Product Rules

1. Never pretend estimated data is measured data.
2. Never let an LLM independently determine irrigation requirements.
3. Never treat all vegetation as equivalent.
4. Never treat all soil as equivalent.
5. Never treat all irrigation systems as equivalent.
6. Never fabricate precision.
7. Never manufacture alerts.
8. Never automatically diagnose plant disease.
9. Never allow low-confidence intelligence to control physical infrastructure.
10. Never overwrite user-confirmed property knowledge without evidence.
11. Never repeatedly ask the user for information already known.
12. Never hard-code the system around one external provider.
13. Never claim water savings without measured evidence.
14. Never allow machine learning to override agronomic safety constraints.
15. Always show the reason behind important recommendations.
16. Always retain historical property observations.
17. Always distinguish measured, estimated, inferred and user-confirmed data.
18. Always allow manual override.
19. Always provide a conservative fallback when critical data is missing.
20. The property is the persistent unit of intelligence.
